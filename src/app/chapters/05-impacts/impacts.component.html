When the upwelling stops

The sea off Peru feeds the world, until it warms

In a normal year, winds drag cold, nutrient-rich water up from the deep along the Peruvian coast. It feeds the anchoveta, the largest single-species fishery on Earth, ground into fishmeal for farms worldwide.

El Niño caps that cold water with a warm, nutrient-poor layer. The fish scatter or move deeper, and the catch falls. In 1972–73, with the fishery already pushed hard, it collapsed.
